Today's goals

  • Read four poems: “The Weary Blues,” “Democracy,” “Caged Bird” and “On Being Brought from Africa to America.”
  • Identify each speaker's attitude toward freedom, voice and belonging.
  • Compare two poems using evidence from the text.
  • Notice how craft (imagery, repetition, rhythm, tone) shapes meaning.

Slide 3

Meet the poets

LANGSTON HUGHES“The Weary Blues,” “Democracy”

Poet of the Harlem Renaissance. Draws on jazz and blues rhythms and speaks plainly about equality.

MAYA ANGELOU“Caged Bird”

Contrasts a free bird and a caged bird to explore freedom and hope.

PHILLIS WHEATLEY“On Being Brought from Africa to America”

An enslaved poet in colonial America who published a book of poems in 1773.

Slide 4

Read each poem three ways

  1. Listen: read it aloud. Where does the rhythm speed up or slow down?
  2. Picture: what images or comparisons stand out?
  3. Decide: who is speaking, and how do they feel about freedom?

Question for today: what does freedom sound like in each poem?

Slide 5

How to compare two poems

  1. Topic: what do both poems talk about?
  2. Difference: how do the speakers' tone or attitude differ?
  3. Evidence: pick a short quotation from each poem.
  4. Craft: name one technique each poet uses.

Sentence starter: “Both poems show ___, but [Poet A] uses ___ while [Poet B] uses ___.”
